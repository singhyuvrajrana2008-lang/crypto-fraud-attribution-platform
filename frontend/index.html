Background

The trail goes cold fast.

Cyber fraud victims increasingly report suspect cryptocurrency wallet addresses used by fraudsters to collect funds, in cases involving investment scams, task-based frauds, sextortion, ransomware, phishing, darknet transactions, and organized cyber-enabled financial crime. During investigations, the reported wallets are usually non-custodial, temporary burner, or intermediary wallets used for layering and laundering — and the inability to quickly identify the associated exchange or VASP delays freezing, evidence preservation, and victim fund recovery.

Non-custodial wallets

No KYC record to start from — attribution has to come from on-chain behaviour, not a registry.

Burner wallets

Created for a single collection event, then abandoned — the address itself is disposable.

Layering wallets

Intermediary hops designed to break the visible link between victim deposit and cash-out point.

Multi-chain / DeFi / mixers

Multi-chain transfers, DeFi protocols, mixers/tumblers, bridges, and privacy-enhancing mechanisms fragment the trail beyond manual tracing.

Fraud typologies in scope

One reported address, many possible cases.

Investment scams Task-based frauds Sextortion Ransomware Phishing Darknet transactions Organized cyber-enabled financial crime
How it works

From complaint to actionable intelligence.

The system follows a fixed investigative sequence — each stage narrows a raw address down to a concrete, evidentiary lead.

01

Ingest

Wallet addresses reported through cybercrime complaint systems — including SAHYOG and NCRP — are pulled in automatically, no manual re-entry.

02

Trace

Automated blockchain tracing walks the transaction graph outward from the reported address across supported chains.

03

Cluster

Exchange wallet clustering and heuristic analysis surface the nearest exchange or VASP receiving direct deposits.

04

Detect

Intermediary and laundering wallets, cross-chain bridges, and fund-movement patterns are flagged along the path.

05

Score

AI/ML-assisted risk detection categorizes wallets and ranks the fraud typology the pattern resembles.

06

Alert

Automated alerts and investigative recommendations are generated for the assigned investigator.

07

Report

A standardized investigation report is produced for evidence preservation and VASP coordination.

Real-Time Identification of Fraud-Linked Cryptocurrency Exchanges from Victim-Reported Suspect Wallet Addresses through Automated Blockchain Analytics

BACKGROUND
Cyber fraud victims increasingly report suspect cryptocurrency wallet addresses used by fraudsters for collection of funds in cases involving:
• investment scams
• task-based frauds
• sextortion
• ransomware
• phishing
• darknet transactions
• organized cyber-enabled financial crimes

During investigations, the reported wallet addresses are often:
• non-custodial wallets
• temporary burner wallets
• or intermediary wallets used for layering and laundering

The inability to quickly identify the cryptocurrency exchange or VASP associated with these wallets delays:
• freezing of assets
• preservation of evidence
• tracing of fund flows
• and victim fund recovery

Manual blockchain tracing requires significant technical expertise and time, particularly in cases involving multi-chain transfers, DeFi protocols, mixers/tumblers, bridges, and privacy-enhancing mechanisms.

DESCRIPTION
The proposed solution envisages a Real-Time Crypto Fraud Attribution System capable of automatically analyzing victim-reported wallet addresses and identifying the nearest exchange or VASP receiving direct deposits.

The system should:
• ingest wallet addresses reported through cybercrime complaint systems
• automatically perform blockchain tracing
• identify associated exchanges or VASPs
• detect fund movement patterns
• generate actionable intelligence for investigators

Key features may include: blockchain transaction graph analysis, clustering of exchange wallets, detection of intermediary laundering wallets, identification of cross-chain fund movement, integration with SAHYOG and NCRP platforms, automated alert generation, and risk categorization of wallets.

The system should support multiple blockchain ecosystems and provide real-time tracing capability, automated investigative recommendations, and analytics dashboards for law enforcement agencies.

EXPECTED SOLUTION
A software platform capable of: real-time blockchain intelligence generation, automated VASP identification, tracing of suspect wallets, cross-chain transaction analytics, fund-flow visualization, integration with LEA systems, and generation of standardized investigation reports.

The system should reduce response time in cyber fraud investigations, improve freezing of proceeds of crime, enhance coordination with VASPs, and strengthen digital evidence collection capabilities.

The platform should further support API integrations, scalable blockchain indexing, AI/ML-assisted risk detection, and automated pattern recognition for fraud typologies.
